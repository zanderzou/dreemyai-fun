Resume from the last thing that happened and mark the pending decision in a separate sentence. Dreemy's FAQ describes chat history for signed-in users. Opening an older conversation gives you a place to reread its last exchange before writing a short continuation.

Imagine your original character Inez has arrived at a pier with a blue ticket. The boat is still tied up. You had paused before deciding whether to board or wait for the harbor keeper. Carry those facts forward without supplying a new outcome.

The ticket is already in Inez's hand. Both the boat and bench are available when the next reply starts.

## Use three lines at the top of the next turn

> Inez is at the pier with her blue ticket.
>
> The boat is still alongside the dock.
>
> The choice is open. Ask whether I want to board or wait for the harbor keeper, then pause.

Select facts that affect the possible actions. The ticket matters because it gives the reader the option to board. The details of an earlier meal can stay in the earlier messages.

## Read what the reply treats as settled

A good continuation acknowledges both choices and ends before either action. If the reply moves the boat out to sea, restate the open decision and replace just that assumption. When you decide to board, write the action as your turn and check that the next reply uses it as a settled fact.
