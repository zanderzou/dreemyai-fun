**Visibility and processing permission are different questions.** Dreemy's [privacy policy](https://www.dreemy.ai/privacy) describes how interaction data is processed. Its [terms](https://www.dreemy.ai/terms) add a separate condition for content set to public. Read the two scopes together rather than assuming one label answers everything.

## General interaction-data processing

The privacy policy identifies inputs and outputs as interaction data and describes service delivery and model improvement. It also refers to anonymized interaction data. Those are provider statements about intended use, not an independent demonstration of the anonymization method or the treatment of a specific account.

A private content setting does not logically negate a separate general processing statement. Likewise, an anonymization claim does not by itself explain which fields are removed or how re-identification risk is assessed.

## The public-content condition

The terms describe additional broad permissions for content made public, including use for improvement, distribution and related purposes. That condition concerns an explicit visibility choice; it should not be paraphrased as a promise that public material is visible only to its creator.

An existing public item and an unpublished item may therefore raise different scope questions. Preserve which state the item actually had, and when, rather than assuming a later visibility change automatically resolves every earlier permission.

## Keep three observations distinct

| Observation | What it can help establish |
| --- | --- |
| Current visibility setting | The state the interface currently shows |
| Applicable published policy | What the provider says it may do |
| Actual processing evidence | What happened to identified data in practice |

The first two do not constitute a complete audit of the third. We did not inspect internal storage, train a model or test an account-level exclusion control.

## Use a narrow clarification request

If the issue matters to existing material, ask which processing applies to private and public categories, whether an exclusion control exists and what a later change affects. Keep future processing, retained records and content already used as separate questions.

Do not upload additional personal material as a test. A minimal description of the category and current setting can frame the question without increasing the amount of data involved.

The same caution applies to deletion: an access change and a processing change are not automatically identical. Preserve any provider confirmation with the specific category and scope it names.

This guide explains the published distinction, not an individual legal entitlement or a method for bypassing eligibility restrictions. The useful conclusion is limited and concrete: public visibility has its own stated permissions, while general interaction-data processing requires its own reading and account-specific clarification where necessary.
