Describe the intended frame before expanding the scene. If the whole character and a tall prop matter, say that explicitly rather than assuming a broad setting will keep every edge visible.

Dreemy's FAQ describes character image generation. It does not guarantee that a prompt will preserve every requested object or crop. The method here is a practical brief and review process for original adult fictional images.

## Decide what must remain visible

For a terrace illustration, choose one character and one closed yellow umbrella. The important elements are the head, both sandals and the complete umbrella tip.

If the shoes are irrelevant to your intended use, a closer frame might be a better choice. Select the crop for the job rather than demanding full-body framing for every picture.

## Write a compact framing instruction

An example brief is: “Original fictional adult woman on a quiet sea terrace, standing with one closed yellow umbrella beside her. Full figure, complete head and both sandals visible, umbrella entirely inside the image, with clear space around the top and bottom edges.”

Add the desired clothes, light and style after that core instruction. Keep those details consistent with the subject and the platform's current rules.

Avoid adding a second tall prop, a second person and several competing foreground objects while you are still trying to solve the framing problem.

## Inspect the returned image itself

Check the outer edges first. Is the umbrella tip complete? Are both sandals actually visible? Is an important hand cut by the canvas boundary?

Then inspect the grip and the relationship between the hand and umbrella. A complete silhouette alone does not establish convincing anatomy.

If a revision is needed, change the framing request or simplify the prop arrangement before adding decorative details. Keep an earlier useful version separately.

This guide does not promise a particular generation success rate, editing control or credit cost. It gives you visible acceptance criteria so you can choose an image that fits its intended article or character scene.
