Give the material and light their own phrases. “An adult fictional tailor wearing a cobalt linen jacket, with a warm window light from the left” specifies fabric, color and direction. If you write “shiny blue” in one place, it can describe silk, gloss on the fabric or the angle of the light.

Dreemy's FAQ describes generating pictures during character chats. Use this approach as a text brief wherever the available image controls let you describe the scene.

The drawn swatches show two different ways blue fabric can catch light. The smooth blue folds have a narrow bright highlight. The darker fabric shows a soft, visible weave.

## Decide what you want the eye to see

Pick a finish and describe one clue. For linen, name the visible weave on the lapel. For satin, place a narrow highlight along the fold of the sleeve. Put the light direction in the same sentence on each attempt so you can compare the material description.

## Change the part that went missing

Look at the final image at its intended size. If the sleeve reads as glossy but you wanted a muted finish, retain the character, pose and window direction. Change the fabric phrase to something observable, such as “matte linen with a visible weave on the sleeve.” If the only issue is a dark fold, change the lighting phrase and reuse the material description. Whether you try again can depend on what the current image tools offer and what the picture already communicates.
